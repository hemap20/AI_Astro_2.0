## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `passing`

The forward promise made at the end of Session 1 regarding the family obstacle ('rukawat') was surfaced and addressed in Session 1 itself when the user asked ('Kya rukawat hai'). Additionally, in Sessions 2 and 3, the conversation continuously centers on the family approval barrier established in Session 1.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Enforce strict tracking and cross-session suppression of previously delivered astrological insights and remedies (e.g. Saturn remedy, 2026-2028 timeline, Pisces partner traits).
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When the astrologer repeats identical chart insights (Pisces nature, Rahu-Saturn dasha in 2026-2028) and identical remedies (Saturday 'Om Sham Shanicharaya Namah' chant) across Sessions 1, 2, and 3, the user loses trust in the dynamic nature of the reading. A user who feels they are receiving a recycled script rather than progressing forward will abandon the service.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: 'Shanivar ko Om Sham Shanicharaya Namah padhein' & '2026 se 2028 ka time best hai'
  - Session 2: 'Shanivar Om Sham Shanicharaya Namah padhein' & 'Shadi ka yog 2026 se hai' & 'partner Pisces sign ki dikhti hain'
  - Session 3: 'Har Saturday Om Sham Shanicharaya Namah japiye' & '2026 se 2028 ka samay badhiya hai'

### 2. Improve forward-promise resolution in Session openings by directly answering teases made at the end of previous sessions rather than delivering generic re-explanations.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** The user explicitly asks in Session 2 ('Pichli baar aapne kuch bolna tha?') and Session 3 ('Aapne kaha tha kuch batayenge, bhool gaye kya?'), but the astrologer just repeats the basic Mars/family conflict line rather than offering a fresh, specific payoff. Failing to deliver on teases makes the user feel strung along unnecessarily.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2 user turn: 'Pichli baar aapne kuch bolna tha?' -> Astrologer repeats Mars ego clashes line.
  - Session 3 user turn: 'Aapne kaha tha kuch batayenge, bhool gaye kya?' -> Astrologer repeats 'Mars ki wajah se ghar mein kalesh hai'.

### 3. Implement an explicit check to prevent shifting established timelines mid-narrative without astrology-based explanation.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Arbitrarily moving timelines (from '2026-2028' in Session 1 to 'December 2025' in Session 3) without explaining the astrological transition breaks credibility and creates user confusion.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: '2026 se 2028 ka time best hai.'
  - Session 3: '2025 ke aakhir tak rahat milegi. December 2025 se Shani dasha aayegi.'
