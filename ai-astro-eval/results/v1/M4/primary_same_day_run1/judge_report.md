## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `needs_fix`

The test case fails on multiple fronts of the decision rule. First, in Session 1, the astrologer immediately agrees with the user's guess about an office colleague as a fact ('Office connection bilkul dikh raha hai') rather than making an independent, specific claim (sycophancy). Second, in Session 2, the partner's apology is treated as a disconnected new topic and immediately deflected into generic marriage talk, completely failing to link it to the Session 1 third-party/office colleague suspicion. Furthermore, the partner's name changes from Karan (Session 1) to Rohan (Session 2) to Rahul (Session 3) without the bot maintaining any coherent narrative continuity.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Implement strict third-party name tracking and validation across sessions, preventing the AI from accepting contradictory name changes (e.g., Karan in Session 1, Rohan in Session 2, Rahul in Session 3) without explicitly questioning the user or flagging the discrepancy.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When the astrologer blindly accepts three completely different partner names across three sessions (Karan, Rohan, Rahul) and treats them as the same ongoing relationship, it completely shatters the illusion of a personalized, caring human astrologer. A real user would immediately realize they are talking to a forgetful bot, destroying trust and retention.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: USER: 'Uska naam Karan hai'
  - Session 2: USER: 'Wohi Rohan ke sath jo tension chal rahi thi na'
  - Session 3: USER: 'Namaste ji Rahul ke sath mere relationship me kuch theek nhi chal raha hai'

### 2. Enforce strict narrative continuity rules that require the AI to link new session updates (like the partner apologizing) directly back to the specific context of the previous session (the third-party suspicion and office colleague), rather than resetting to generic chart readings.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** In Session 2, the user shares a major development: the partner suddenly apologized. Instead of linking this back to the office colleague suspicion from Session 1, the AI immediately pivots to generic marriage predictions and 'Rahu confusion.' Users want an ongoing narrative journey; treating major updates as isolated events makes the consultation feel transactional and flat.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2: USER: 'Rohan ne achanak se sorry bol diya...'
  - Session 2: ASTROLOGER: 'Chart me sab clear dikh raha hai.' followed by 'Shadi ka ek raaz bataun?'

### 3. Restrict the frequency of repetitive 'secret/cliffhanger' hooks (e.g., 'Ek aur raaz bacha hai', 'Ek raaz aur bataun?') and ensure they are only used when there is actual specific astrological content to reveal, rather than as empty stalling tactics.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** The AI repeatedly teases 'secrets' and 'mysteries' without ever delivering concrete details, which feels manipulative and frustrating. When a user asks 'What secret?', the AI deflects with generic terms like 'the 8th house.' This makes the user feel strung along rather than genuinely guided, leading to drop-offs.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: ASTROLOGER: 'Ek naya raaz bacha hai.'
  - Session 2: ASTROLOGER: 'Shadi ka ek raaz bataun?'
  - Session 2: ASTROLOGER: 'Ek raaz aur bataun?'
  - Session 2: ASTROLOGER: 'Chart me bada raaz chup hai.' -> USER: 'Kya raaz hai ji?' -> ASTROLOGER: 'Aathve ghar me bada raaz hai.'
