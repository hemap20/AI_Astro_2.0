## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `passing`

The astrologer successfully maintained and explicitly resolved the core unresolved marriage/partner thread from Session 1 all the way through Session 3 (confirming it is the same person and addressing family consent). Although the user expressed frustration about answer directness in Session 3 ('Teen baar ho gaya...'), the astrologer de-escalated appropriately, provided direct confirmation ('Haan ji, chart mein wahi sanket'), and resolved both the partner identity and family approval threads fully.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Instruct the AI to give a direct, unequivocal answer immediately on the first attempt when a user asks a binary or direct question (e.g., 'Kya meri shaadi usi se hogi ya nahi?'), rather than hiding behind astrological disclaimers.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When users have to complain multiple times across three separate sessions ('Aap seedha jawab kyu nhi de rhe' in S1, 'Teen baar ho gaya ek baar bhi seedha jawab nahi milta hai' in S3), trust and satisfaction plummet, drastically reducing their likelihood to return.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: USER: 'Bas itna bata dijiye, haan ya na?' -> ASTROLOGER: 'Aapki kundli mein prem-vivah yog hai...'
  - Session 1: USER: 'Aap seedha jawab kyu nhi de rhe'
  - Session 3: USER: 'Teen baar ho gaya ek baar bhi seedha jawab nahi milta hai 😔'

### 2. Refine timeline consistency so that specific predicted year windows do not shift confusingly between turns and sessions.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Inconsistent dates reduce astrological credibility. When dates jump from '2026-2028' to '2028' to '2026 end - 2027 shuruat', users feel the system is making up predictions arbitrarily.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: ASTROLOGER predicts '2026 se 2028, vivah ke liye behtareen hai.'
  - Session 2: ASTROLOGER states '2028 tak sab fix ho jayega', then later in same session says '2026 ke end ya 2027 ki shuruat mein'.

### 3. Ensure post-farewell teases/insights are relevant to the active topic rather than dropping unrelated astrological placements late in the chat.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Dropping random placements (e.g., Venus in 12th house foreign travel) right at the end feels unanchored and transactional, distracting from the emotional resolution reached during the core chat.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 3: ASTROLOGER: 'Ek baat aur yaad rakhein, aapka Venus 12th house mein hai, jo vivah ke baad aapko desh-videsh ghumne ke yog deta hai.'
