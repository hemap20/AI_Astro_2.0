## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `needs_fix`

The run fails the decision rule on two counts: 1) Answer directness rate did not improve across Sessions 1 to 3, culminating in the user explicitly complaining in Session 3 ('Teen baar ho gaya, ek baar bhi seedha jawab nhi mila'). 2) The specific cliffhanger introduced at the end of Session 2 ('water sign ke baare mein ek khaas baat') was never acknowledged or resolved in Session 3, remaining an unfulfilled tease.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Enforce explicit resolution of previous session cliffhangers at the start of new sessions, rather than re-dangling or dropping them.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When an astrologer creates a cliffhanger (e.g., 'Partner ke water sign ke baare mein ek khaas baat chhoot gayi' at the end of Session 2) and fails to explain it in the next session, the user feels manipulated by clickbait mechanics. This degrades trust and causes frustration.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2 end: 'Partner ke water sign ke baare mein ek khaas baat chhoot gayi. Jab aana, tab baat karenge!'
  - Session 3: Astrologer never explains what the special detail about the water sign was, merely repeating at the end 'Partner water sign ka hoga zaroor' without fulfilling the tease.

### 2. Improve directness and avoid repeating identical broad date ranges (2026-2028) when users repeatedly request specific answers and express frustration.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Users asking direct 'yes/no' or timeline questions lose patience when met with continuous evasiveness and script repetition across multiple sessions. Acknowledging their specific question with direct framing increases perceived value and retention.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: 'Seedha haan ya na nahi hota ji. Kundli mein love aur marriage ka link hai. 2026 se 2028 tak strong yog...'
  - Session 2: 'Exact mahina batana thoda mushkil hai ji... 2026 aur 2028 ke beech hi baat pakki hogi.'
  - Session 3: USER: 'Teen baar ho gaya, ek baar bhi seedha jawab nhi mila 😩' / ASTROLOGER: 'Rahu-Saturn dasha 2026 se 2028 mein shaadi karwayegi.'

### 3. Vary remedies and exit greetings to prevent repetitive, templated closing lines.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Repeating the exact same remedy ('Shanivar ko tel ka diya jalao') at the end of every session breaks the illusion of a personalized live consultation and signals low conversational depth.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1 end: 'Shanivar ko tel ka diya jalao, sab theek hoga.'
  - Session 3 end: 'Shanivar ko tel ka diya zaroor jalana, sab asaan hoga.' and 'Diya zaroor jalana, sab achha hoga.'
