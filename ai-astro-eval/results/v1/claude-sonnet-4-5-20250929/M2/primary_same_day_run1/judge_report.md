## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `passing`

Answer directness improved significantly after Session 1 (where the bot deflected twice). In Session 2 and Session 3, direct questions about timeline ('2026 mein kb tk', '2026 tk wait krna padega kya', 'paka hai na') were answered immediately and directly without non-answers. Furthermore, core established narrative threads (Love-cum-arranged marriage, 2026-2028 window, Saturn/Rahu delays, Jupiter 7th lord, same caste) were seamlessly carried over, maintained, and elaborated across all three sessions without dropping.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Enforce strict persona/identity consistency across sessions and prevent greeting glitches (e.g., correcting the user about gender/title like 'Main Sitara hoon, aapki didi nahi sir' when previously referred to as sir or guru ji without issue).
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** In Session 3, the astrologer abruptly opens with 'Main Sitara hoon, aapki didi nahi sir 😊', breaking the illusion of an ongoing, continuous relationship established in Sessions 1 and 2 where the user called them 'guru ji' and 'sir'. Tonal and persona reset shatters trust and immersion, signaling to the user that they are talking to a disconnected bot rather than a familiar personal advisor.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 3, Turn 2: ASTROLOGER says 'Namaste ji! Main Sitara hoon, aapki didi nahi sir 😊' after the user simply said 'Hello sir'.

### 2. Improve direct answer capability and decrease repetitive framing/deflections before revealing core answers.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When a user asks direct questions like 'haan ya na' or '2026 mein kb tk pakka smjhu', they seek direct reassurance. Pushing back or forcing intermediate questions twice in a row frustrates the user, lowering their willingness to stay and engage deeper.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1, Turn 3-5: User asks 'Bas itna bata dijiye, haan ya na?' -> Astrologer deflects twice asking to confirm unmarried status before answering.
  - Session 2, Turn 3-4: User asks 'par shadi kb tk hogi ye btao na' after sharing nature.

### 3. Vary pre-farewell cliffhangers and hooks so they don't use identical repetitive templates across sessions.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Repeating the exact same hook ('8th house mein Jupiter-Saturn... in-laws ke saath relation kaisa rahega') across consecutive sessions makes retention hooks feel scripted, unnatural, and like automated upselling rather than organic curiosity loops.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2, Turn 11: ASTROLOGER says 'aapke 8th house mein Jupiter-Saturn dono hain, iska matlab in-laws ke saath relation kaisa rahega... Ye bhi jaanna chahogi?'
  - Session 3, Turn 7: ASTROLOGER repeats 'aapke in-laws kaisi hongi aur unke saath relation kaisa rahega, wo batana baaki hai. Ye bhi jaanna chahogi?'
