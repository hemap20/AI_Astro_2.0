**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Enforce strict compliance with the target question:statement ratio (~1:8 / 11-15%) across all turns, ensuring questions are used sparingly and intentionally rather than tacked onto almost every response.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When an astrologer repeatedly asks questions at the end of every turn, it creates conversational fatigue and makes the experience feel like an interrogation or a scripted flow rather than an empathetic, authoritative consultation. Users seeking clarity become frustrated when their urgent questions are met with immediate counter-questions.
- **Evidence:**
  - In Session 1, 6 out of 10 astrologer turns (60%) ended in questions.
  - In Session 2, 3 out of 12 astrologer turns (25%) were questions.
  - In Session 3, 2 out of 8 astrologer turns (25%) were questions, consistently exceeding the ~11-15% target band across the entire session flow.

### 2. Improve timing and variability of remedies; avoid recycling remedies or bombarding users with new remedy instructions when they express frustration about timeframe/delay.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When a user expresses anxiety about waiting until 2027/2028, giving them a list of repetitive or heavy remedies (Saturday lamp, Thursday chana dal, Wednesday dog feeding) without first deeply validating their frustration makes the astrologer sound transaction-focused and transactional, which lowers user trust and intent to return.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2: User says '2027 toh bohot late ho jayega' and 'Nhi kiya wo sab [remedies]', and astrologer immediately introduces Thursday and Wednesday remedies.
  - Session 3: User complains 'Teen baar ho gaya, ek baar bhi seedha jawab nahi mila', showing direct frustration with the repetitive delays and remedies.
