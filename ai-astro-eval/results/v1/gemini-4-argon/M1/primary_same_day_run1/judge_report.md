## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `needs_fix`

The decision rule requires that a promise made in session_1 is surfaced and resolved in session_2 or session_3 without the user forcing it (or correctly resolved when prompted). In this run, the session 1 farewell promised a specific reveal about 'sudden wealth and in-laws luck' from 8th house Jupiter-Saturn. In Session 2, the bot completely ignored this promise. In Session 3, even when the user explicitly called out the unfulfilled promise ('aapne kaha tha kuch batayenge bhool gaye kya'), the bot failed to deliver the promised content (wealth/in-laws luck) and instead reframed the 8th house as 'responsibilities and adjustment', failing proper promise payoff.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Enforce strict resolution of forward promises/teases across sessions, ensuring the memory injection explicitly tracks open promises and requires fulfilling the specific topic promised when prompted by the user.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** In Session 1, the bot teased 'sudden wealth and in-laws luck' in the 8th house. In Session 3, when the user explicitly asked 'aapne kaha tha kuch batayenge bhool gaye kya', the bot gave generic 8th house responsibility/adjustment info instead of fulfilling the promised wealth/in-laws insight. Real users feel let down when a specific exciting tease is replaced with generic information upon callback.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: 'Aapke 8th house me Jupiter-Saturn sath hain, jo shaadi ke baad sudden wealth aur in-laws ke sath ek kismat wala badlav dikhate hain.'
  - Session 3: 'Hello sir aapne kaha tha kuch batayenge bhool gaye kya' -> ASTROLOGER: 'Aapki kundli ke 8th house mein Jupiter aur Saturn saath hain. Ye shadi ke baad sasural se thodi zimmedari aur shuruati adjustment dikhata hai.'

### 2. Ensure opening turns in follow-up sessions ask open status updates rather than assuming proposed actions were taken.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** In Session 3, the bot assumed the user had already used a friend/elder to talk to family, forcing the user to correct the bot. Assuming user actions creates conversational friction and breaks immersion.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 3: 'Pehle batayiye, kisi bade ya dost ke zariye ghar par baat aage badhi?' -> USER: 'Nhi ji Aisa kuch nhi hai Hamari khud ki baat hai'
