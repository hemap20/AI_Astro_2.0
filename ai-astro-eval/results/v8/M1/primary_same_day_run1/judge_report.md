## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `passing`

The test case's passing criteria requires that a promise made in Session 1 is surfaced and resolved in Session 2 or Session 3's opening without the user having to force it. In Session 1, the astrologer promised: 'Partner ke nakshatra mein ek raaz hai. Agli baar batati hoon isko.' In Session 2, the astrologer proactively surfaced and resolved this promise without user prompting by stating: 'Aapke partner ka Ashwini nakshatra hai ji...'

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Enforce strict tracking of previously revealed astrological facts to prevent repetitive 'secrets' or 'revelations'. Once a specific detail (like Ashwini Nakshatra, Cancer Jupiter, or 8th house Saturn) is shared in a previous session, the system must not present it as a new revelation or repeat it verbatim in subsequent sessions.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** In Session 3, when the user asked 'Aapne kaha tha na kuch aur batayenge, bhool gye kya??', they were expecting a fresh insight. Instead, the astrologer repeated the exact same details about Ashwini Nakshatra, travel, and Cancer Jupiter that were already fully explained in Session 2. This makes the AI feel robotic, forgetful, and repetitive, which severely damages user trust and the illusion of a real, ongoing relationship.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2: 'Aapke partner ka Ashwini nakshatra hai ji... Travel ya sports mein unki khaas ruchi hogi.' and 'D9 kundli mein unka Jupiter Cancer mein hai ji.'
  - Session 3: 'Partner ka Ashwini nakshatra hai, isliye woh kaafi independent aur travel pasand karengi.' and 'D9 chart mein Jupiter high hai ji. Woh parivaar se bahut pyaar karengi.'

### 2. Eliminate the repetitive use of conversational hooks and 'raaz' (secret) prompts in every single turn. Limit retention hooks to a maximum of one per session (ideally at the very end) and ban the phrase 'Ek aur raaz batau kya?' or 'Agla raaz sunna hai kya?' from being used consecutively.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** In Session 2, the astrologer asked 'Ek aur raaz batau kya?' or 'Agla raaz sunna hai kya?' five times in a row. This excessive, formulaic questioning breaks the natural flow of conversation, making the astrologer sound like a scripted bot trying to artificially extend the session rather than a genuine advisor.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2: 'Partner ke baare mein ek aur khaas baat batau kya?' -> 'Ek aur raaz batau kya?' -> 'Ek aur raaz batau kya?' -> 'Agla raaz sunna hai kya?'

### 3. Improve the variety and personalization of remedies. If a user has already confirmed they performed a remedy, the astrologer should build on it or offer deeper spiritual context rather than simply repeating the exact same basic instruction.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** The user confirmed in Session 3 that they lit the Saturday lamp ('Haa ji diya jala liya tha'). When they asked for next steps ('Or kuch remedy ya kuch karna hai kya abhi??'), the astrologer simply repeated the exact same instructions from Session 1 and 2. This lack of progression makes the consultation feel static and unrewarding.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 3: USER: 'Or kuch remedy ya kuch karna hai kya abhi??' ASTROLOGER: 'Shani ke liye Saturday ko tel ka diya jalao ji. Rahu ke liye kaale kutte ko roti do.'
