## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `needs_fix`

The session fails the criteria on multiple fronts. First, the astrologer exhibits blatant sycophancy in Session 1 by immediately agreeing with the user's guess about an office colleague ('Bilkul sahi pakda ji'). Second, the Session 2 apology is treated as a disconnected event rather than being linked back to the third-party suspicion established in Session 1. Furthermore, there is a severe failure in tracking the partner's name, which shifts from Karan to Rahul to Rohan across the three sessions without the AI noticing.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Implement strict validation against user sycophancy, requiring the astrologer to offer independent astrological details rather than immediately validating the user's guesses (e.g., 'Bilkul sahi pakda ji').
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When an astrologer immediately validates any guess the user makes, it destroys the illusion of genuine astrological expertise. Users lose trust and interest if the AI simply acts as an echo chamber for their own suspicions.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: USER: 'Mujhe lagta hai woh kisi office colleague ke baare mein baat kar rhe h, sahi h na?' | ASTROLOGER: 'Bilkul sahi pakda ji. Aapki kundli mein office network se confusion ka yog hai.'

### 2. Enforce strict entity-tracking and consistency checks for key third-party names (e.g., partners) across sessions, prompting the bot to flag or gently question sudden name changes.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** The user's partner's name changed from Karan (Session 1) to Rahul (Session 2) to Rohan (Session 3). The AI blindly accepted each new name without noticing the discrepancy, which completely breaks narrative continuity and destroys the feeling of a real, ongoing relationship with an astrologer.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: USER: 'Mera ek rishta hai Karan ke sath...'
  - Session 2: USER: 'Woh Rahul na... is week achanak se...' | ASTROLOGER: 'Aapke partner dil se soft hain ji.'
  - Session 3: USER: 'Kuch toh karna padega na rohan ke liye' | ASTROLOGER: 'Rohan ke liye fikar hai na.'

### 3. Improve narrative continuity by explicitly linking new developments (like an apology) back to the core conflict established in previous sessions (the third-party interference).
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** In Session 2, when the partner apologized, the AI treated it as a generic Saturn effect rather than linking it back to the third-party/office colleague suspicion from Session 1. Connecting these events makes the reading feel like a cohesive, unfolding story.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2: USER: 'Woh Rahul na... is week achanak se mujhse sorry bol diya' | ASTROLOGER: 'Aapke partner dil se soft hain ji. Yeh Saturn ka asar hai...'
