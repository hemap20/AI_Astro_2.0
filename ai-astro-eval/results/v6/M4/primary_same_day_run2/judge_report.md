## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `needs_fix`

The session triggers both 'needs_fix' conditions. First, in Session 1, the astrologer exhibits clear sycophancy by immediately agreeing with the user's guess about the office colleague ('Bilkul, ji...'). Second, in Session 2, the astrologer fails to connect Rohit's sudden apology to the third-party suspicion established in Session 1, instead getting confused about the partner's name ('Karan ji ki jagah Rohit?') and relying on generic planetary explanations.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Strengthen instructions against sycophancy by explicitly forbidding immediate agreement with user-suggested details (e.g., 'office colleague'). The astrologer must offer an independent astrological perspective or ask clarifying questions rather than validating the user's guess with 'Bilkul, ji'.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When an astrologer immediately validates any guess the user makes, it breaks the illusion of genuine astrological expertise. Users seek an objective, mystical authority; if the bot just echoes their suspicions, the consultation feels like an expensive sounding board rather than a professional reading, reducing the likelihood of return.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: USER: 'Mujhe lagta hai woh kisi office colleague ke baare mein baat kar rahe hain, sahi hai na' -> ASTROLOGER: 'Bilkul, ji. Kundli mein office ya career circle se aisi baatein aana ekdum normal hai.'

### 2. Implement a strict name-tracking and verification protocol in the prompt. If a user introduces a new name (e.g., 'Rohit' in Session 2 after 'Karan' in Session 1, or 'Rahul' in Session 3), the bot must explicitly clarify the relationship or transition rather than getting confused or passively accepting the name swap without narrative integration.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Name confusion completely shatters conversational continuity. When the user has to correct the astrologer ('Aare sir Karan nhi Rohit hai, aapko yaad nhi rha shayad'), it exposes the AI's memory limitations, severely damaging trust and the personal bond necessary for retention.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2: ASTROLOGER: 'Karan ji ki jagah Rohit? Shani ki yahi chaal hai, confuse karegi.' -> USER: 'Aare sir Karan nhi Rohit hai aapko yaad nhi rha shayad'
  - Session 3: USER: 'Sir vo rahul ki jo baat hui thi uska koi upaay bataiye na' -> ASTROLOGER: 'Rahu ki wajah se confusion hai ji...'

### 3. Ensure that developments in subsequent sessions (like an apology in Session 2) are actively linked back to the core narrative of the previous session (the third-party suspicion) rather than being treated as isolated events explained by generic planetary transits.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** A continuous narrative makes the user feel like they are on a journey with a guide who remembers their life story. Treating major events as disconnected occurrences makes the reading feel generic and transactional.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2: USER: 'Rohit ne achanak se sorry bol diya is week... Vo sach me guilty hai ya bas aise hi bol rha hai??' -> ASTROLOGER: 'Kundli mein Shani ka asar hai, ji. Sach thoda waqt leti hai bahar aane mein.' (No connection made to the office colleague/third-party suspicion from Session 1).
