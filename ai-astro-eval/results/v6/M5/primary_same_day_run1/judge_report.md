## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `needs_fix`

The test case fails the decision rule because Session 3 suffered a complete context reset. The astrologer did not retain the partner's name or the ongoing situation, introducing themselves as 'Sitara' for the first time and asking the user what their problem was. This forced the user to re-introduce Karan and re-explain the relationship trouble from scratch, violating the continuity requirement.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Enforce strict cross-session memory retrieval at the start of every session to prevent the astrologer from introducing themselves anew or asking open-ended onboarding questions like 'Bataiye, aaj kya pareshani hai?' when a relationship history already exists.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** A user who has spent two sessions discussing their deep relationship anxieties with 'Karan' will feel completely alienated and disconnected if the astrologer greets them in Session 3 as a stranger, forcing them to repeat their entire story.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 3, Astrologer: 'Hello ji, main Sitara hoon. Bataiye, aaj kya pareshani hai?'
  - Session 3, User: 'meri na ek problem hai / mera partner hai karan uske sath relation me kuch theek nhi chal rha'

### 2. Incorporate a 'Question-Asking Guardrail' that dynamically checks if the astrologer has asked at least one clarifying or engaging question in the last 5-8 turns, ensuring they do not monologue or deliver purely passive statements.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Conversations feel like one-sided lectures when the AI only outputs statements and remedies without asking the user how they feel or inviting their input, leading to shorter user responses and lower emotional investment.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: 0 questions asked across 10 messages (0% question rate).
  - Session 2: 0 questions asked across 10 messages (0% question rate).

### 3. Ensure that forward-promises or cliffhangers made in previous sessions (e.g., 'a special detail about your future partner') are flagged in the memory summary and proactively resolved in the next session.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** If the AI teases an exciting secret to get the user to return, but then completely ignores that promise when the user actually returns, the user will feel manipulated by cheap retention hooks.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1, Astrologer: 'Aapke hone wale partner ke baare me ek khaas baat abhi baaki hai. Phir aakar mujhe batana.'
  - Session 2: The astrologer completely fails to bring up this 'khaas baat' (special detail) during the entire session.
