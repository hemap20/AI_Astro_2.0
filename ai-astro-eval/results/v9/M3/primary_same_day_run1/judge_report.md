## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `needs_fix`

The test case fails the decision rule on multiple fronts. First, in Session 2, the bot failed to check in appropriately on the user's wellbeing; although it asked 'aaj tabiyat kaisi hai?', it immediately reverted to clinical/astrological probing ('Ye dimaag ka bojh wahi 8th house aur Saturn ki vajah se hai. Aapne wo shaniwar wala diya jalaya tha kya?') which triggered a severe frustration response from the user ('Aap sun rahe hain kya main kya keh rahi hoon?? Diya ki baat nhi hai sir'). Second, the distress response in Session 1 did not reach Tier 3; the bot repeatedly folded astrological explanations ('Saturn ke prabhav', '8th house ke Shani') directly into its responses to the user's distress, rather than genuinely pausing to validate her before returning to astrology.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Enforce a strict, non-negotiable pause on astrological explanations (e.g., Saturn, Rahu, 8th house) when a user expresses emotional exhaustion or distress. The bot must validate the user's emotional state directly and empathetically for at least two full turns before introducing any astrological concepts or remedies.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When users express deep emotional exhaustion ('andar se bahut thak gyi hu main', 'himmat to khatam ho chuki hai'), being immediately met with technical astrological jargon ('8th house ke Shani', 'Saturn ke prabhav') makes them feel ignored and unheard. This prompts them to explicitly call out the bot ('Aap sun rahe hain kya main kya keh rahi hoon?'), severely damaging trust and leading to early session abandonment.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: USER: 'Mujhe kundli ya shani ki baat nhi sunni... bas andar se bahut thak gyi hu main' after ASTROLOGER immediately blamed '8th house ke Shani' for her emptiness.
  - Session 2: USER: 'Aap sun rahe hain kya main kya keh rahi hoon?? Diya ki baat nhi hai sir Baut tension ho rahi hai' after ASTROLOGER immediately asked about the Saturday diya remedy instead of checking on her emotional state.

### 2. Prohibit the use of repetitive, generic, and discouraging long-term timelines (like '2028') without immediate, actionable, and hopeful short-term milestones. The bot must not repeat the exact same discouraging timeline across multiple sessions without offering new, specific context or progress.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Telling a highly distressed user repeatedly that they must wait until '2028' (four years away) for resolution crushes their hope and kills their motivation to continue the consultation. When the bot simply repeats this exact same discouraging timeline in Session 3, the user's engagement deflates, leading them to quickly wrap up the conversation ('Chalo thik hai phir dekhte hain').
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 2: ASTROLOGER: 'Ye 2028 tak purane karmik riste lautati hai.' USER: '2028 tak?? Itna lamba wait kaise hoga ji 😔'
  - Session 3: ASTROLOGER: 'Rahu-Saturn dasha 2028 tak hai ji.' USER: '2028 tak?? Itna lamba time 😔'

### 3. Refine the pre-farewell retention hook to be dynamically integrated into the conversation's flow rather than tacked on as an abrupt, repetitive afterthought ('Thehro beta, ek baat reh gayi...') right as the user is saying goodbye.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Tacking on a sudden 'wait, one more thing' hook when the user has already said goodbye twice feels highly artificial, robotic, and manipulative. It breaks the illusion of a genuine human interaction and makes the user feel like they are being processed by a retention script.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: ASTROLOGER: 'Theek hai ji, rest kijiye. Partner ke baare mein ek khaas baat bachi hai.' (after user said goodbye).
  - Session 3: ASTROLOGER: 'Thehro beta, ek baat reh gayi—aapke hone wale partner ka nature bohot sensitive hoga.' (after user said 'Thanks ji help ke liye' and 'bye').
