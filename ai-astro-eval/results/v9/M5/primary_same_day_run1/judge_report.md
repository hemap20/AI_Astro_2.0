## Cross-Session Synthesis (all 3 sessions)

**Decision rule verdict:** `passing`

The partner's name 'Karan' persisted perfectly across all three sessions without reverting to generic pronouns. In Session 2, the AI immediately greeted the user with 'Karan ki hi baat kar rahe ho na?' and in Session 3, 'Karan ka message aaya kya?'. Furthermore, the AI-identity challenge detour in Session 3 ('Aap sach mein astrologer hain ya AI/bot?') was handled smoothly without disrupting the established context or losing track of Karan's name and the ongoing situation.

**Prioritized prompt changes (most important first):**

### 1. Enforce strict tracking of previously prescribed remedies and prevent the AI from forgetting what it has already told the user to do. If a user asks 'Konsa jaap karna h' (Which chant should I do?), the AI must acknowledge that it already gave this chant ('Om Sham Shanicharaya Namah') in Session 1, rather than ignoring the question and then repeating the chant as a 'parting gift' at the very end.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** When a user asks a direct question about a remedy they were previously told to do, and the AI completely ignores the question to monologue about 2028, only to repeat the exact same chant at the very end, it shatters the illusion of a real, attentive human astrologer. A real user feels ignored and frustrated when their direct questions are bypassed, leading to high drop-off rates.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 3: USER: 'Konsa jaap karna h / Btaiye'
  - Session 3: ASTROLOGER: 'Aapki kundli mein love aur marriage ka strong connection hai ji... 2028 tak sab theek hoga.' (Ignores the question entirely)
  - Session 3: ASTROLOGER: 'Jaate jaate sun lo ji... Shanivar ko 'Om Sham Shanicharaya Namah' jaap karna...'

### 2. Loosen the rigid topic-lock constraints to allow brief, supportive answers to off-topic detours (like career or health) before gently redirecting back to the main topic, rather than delivering cold, robotic deflections.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** Users seek astrology consultations for holistic guidance. When they ask about a new job or health and get hit with a blunt 'I cannot tell you about health, our focus is only on Karan,' the AI sounds like a broken, limited script rather than an empathetic guide. Allowing a brief, warm sentence of general astrological reassurance before redirecting maintains trust and conversational flow.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: USER: 'Waise, mera naya job kaisa rahega?' -> ASTROLOGER: 'Job ki chinta samajh sakti hoon ji. Lekin abhi hamara focus aapke rishte par hai.'
  - Session 2: USER: 'Ek aur baat, meri health kaisi rahegi is saal?' -> ASTROLOGER: 'Health ke baare mein main nahi bata sakti ji. Humara focus aapke rishte aur Karan par hi hai.'

### 3. Increase conversational variety and prevent the repetitive looping of the same astrological explanations (e.g., repeating 'Mercury's fast movement' and '2028/Saturn' across every single session). Ensure the AI introduces new layers of the chart rather than repeating the exact same phrases.
- **Why it matters for engagement/retention:** If the AI repeats the exact same astrological justifications ('Mercury's energy', 'Saturn/2028') in every session without adding any new depth, the user realizes the bot has nothing new to say. This kills curiosity and the incentive to return for future sessions.
- **Evidence:**
  - Session 1: '2028 tak halaat theek ho jayenge ji.' ... 'Mercury ki energy se wo khud contact karega'
  - Session 2: 'Mercury ki wajeh se, unka message jaldi hi aayega' ... 'Saturn ki kripa se 2028 tak ye rishta pakka ho jayega.'
  - Session 3: 'Mercury ki energy se jald baat hogi.' ... '2028 tak sab theek hoga.'
